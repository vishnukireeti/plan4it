Aerospace Engineer

Designs, tests, and maintains aircraft, spacecraft, satellites, and drones for civil and defence applications.

Who it suits

Physics and maths lovers dreaming about flight, rockets, and space missions.

How to become a Aerospace Engineer

  1. Class 12 (Physics, Chemistry, Maths)
  2. B.Tech Aerospace via JEE
  3. Learn CFD, Structures, Propulsion
  4. Projects with Drones, Rockets, or Student Satellites
  5. Design / Test Engineer at ISRO, HAL, DRDO, or Private Firms

Skills you need

AerodynamicsIntermediate · Technical

Explains lift, drag, and how vehicles move through air.

Where to learn: NPTEL Aerodynamics (free)

CAD & CFD ToolsIntermediate · Tool

Simulates airflow and designs components.

Where to learn: OpenFOAM / SolidWorks Student (free)

Propulsion & StructuresIntermediate · Technical

Core to designing engines and lightweight structures.

Where to learn: NPTEL (free)

  • MATLAB / Python
  • Systems Engineering

Useful certifications

  • Certified Six Sigma Green Belt
  • Systems Engineering Certificate
  • Drone Pilot Certificate (DGCA-approved)

Entrance exams for this career

  • JEE Main & AdvancedEngineering · NTA / IITs
  • JEE AdvancedEngineering · IITs (JEE Advanced 2026 organised by IIT Roorkee)
  • GATEEngineering · IIT Madras (2027)

Good things

  • Work on aircraft, satellites, and space missions
  • Growing private space and drone startups in India
  • Prestigious options at ISRO, HAL, and DRDO

Hard things

  • Few employers so openings are limited
  • Government selection processes are highly competitive
