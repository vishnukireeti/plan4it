Mechanical Engineer

Designs, analyses, and improves machines, engines, and manufacturing systems across automotive, energy, and industrial sectors.

Who it suits

Hands-on problem solvers who enjoy physics, tools, and understanding how machines work.

How to become a Mechanical Engineer

  1. Class 12 (Physics, Chemistry, Maths)
  2. B.Tech Mechanical Engineering
  3. Learn CAD, Simulation, Manufacturing
  4. Internships at Auto or Manufacturing Firms
  5. Graduate Engineer Trainee Role

Skills you need

CAD (SolidWorks / AutoCAD)Intermediate · Tool

The standard way to design and document parts.

Where to learn: SolidWorks / Autodesk Student Licence (free)

Thermodynamics & MechanicsIntermediate · Domain

Core physics behind engines, heat, and motion.

Where to learn: NPTEL (free)

Manufacturing ProcessesBeginner · Domain

Knowing how parts are made keeps designs practical.

Where to learn: NPTEL Manufacturing Courses (free)

  • FEA / Simulation
  • Project Management

Useful certifications

  • Certified SolidWorks Associate (CSWA)
  • Six Sigma Green Belt
  • Autodesk Certified Professional

Entrance exams for this career

  • JEE Main & AdvancedEngineering · NTA / IITs
  • JEE AdvancedEngineering · IITs (JEE Advanced 2026 organised by IIT Roorkee)
  • GATEEngineering · IIT Madras (2027)

Good things

  • Wide range of industries from auto to aerospace
  • Solid foundation for manufacturing and EV careers
  • Strong PSU and government job options

Hard things

  • Core-sector salaries start lower than software
  • Some roles involve plant or site work away from cities
